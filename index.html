Team needs and trade fit

Each team's strengths and weaknesses, its star, and the realistic players who would fill its biggest holes. Realistic is the point: targets are only Rotation / depth, Elite role player, Undervalued / buy-low, or High-value starter players, never a top-3 player on their own team, never a 20-point scorer or a 30-minute-a-night player on their own team. Salaries are shown with the fit on each team page (Basketball-Reference contracts, display only), but they do not change the score. This is a box-score fit, a prior for lineup data, not a lineup prediction. TradeScore is QualityGain plus twice FitGain minus the availability discount, on this run from 0.06 to 0.51, with QualityGain averaging 0.06 and FitGain 0.06, so FitGain drives the score more here; it is a fit ranking, not a prediction and not a pick. Each team page leads with less-common fits: its best targets who do not top many other teams' lists, then the best available role players. Full per-team lists in team_needs_2026_27.csv.

Only 23 distinct players fill the 240 top-overall slots across the 30 teams: the same best available role players top many teams' lists (most often Alex Caruso (29), Paul Reed (28), Jakob Poeltl (25), Aday Mara (24)). That is expected, the best available role players are the best fit for every needy team, but it means the lists read as nearly the same list. Salaries help tell them apart on the team pages: the same fit at $2.85M is a different suggestion than the same fit at $19.55M.

How to read this

Getting around: the bar at the top jumps to the team ratings, the player leaders, the top 100 players, the rookies, or the GM board. East / West tabs above the team table filter to one conference; the filter box searches team names. Click a team name anywhere to open that team's own page, with its ratings, game log, and roster. Click a team row to see its game log and roster for the season right here. The RPR research page ranks the free data sources that could improve RPR, with the proposed formulas, the validation gate, and what is not worth adding.

AdjO / AdjD: points scored and allowed per 100 possessions, adjusted for opponent. The small number under each value is that team's rank in the metric; AdjD rank 1 is the best defense. Net is AdjO minus AdjD, and its rank is the Rk column. AdjT is adjusted tempo, possessions per game. Conf is East or West. W-L is the record in the games these ratings are built from, so W+L equals GP.

ORtg (players) is a simplified approximation: points per 100 possessions used, with no assist credit, so it underrates playmakers. Read it with %Poss. The tables show ORtg (full), the Oliver version with assist credit, falling back to shrunk simple ORtg where the full version is not available. RPR v1.2 is our own single-number player rating, not KenPom's proprietary kPOY: the ORtg that enters it is first shrunk toward the league-average ORtg (K = 200 possessions used) and adjusted for the defences the player faced (OppAdj, shrunk toward 0 with K = 150 possessions faced in November; K = 500 was for a full season and crushed the adjustment to about 0.1 points, so it is re-checked at mid-season). On top of that, v1.2 recenters the counting terms (playmaking, defense, rebounding) on their league-average per-36 rates, with defense at full weight (steals plus blocks at 1.0; v1.3 half-weighted it at 0.5, but that version is kept in parallel, not the headline, since 28 Nov 2026) and no minutes shrink, so an average player scores about 0 from those terms and the median RPR is near 0, instead of every player getting free credit just for playing. There is no minutes floor in v1.2. The parallel v1.3 values are kept as rpr_v13, with the old values rpr_v1 and rpr_v1_1, in the CSVs. RPR_value = RPR v1.2 * GP / 82 is how much value a player has delivered so far; it is not part of the rating. Leaderboards only include players with at least 40% of their team's minutes, so a two-minute cameo cannot top the list. Tradeoff, stated honestly: v1.2 split-half reliability was 0.625, below v1.1's 0.691, while its team aggregation was 0.729 vs 0.67, so v1.2 was more honest at team level and less stable player to player; v1.3, kept in parallel, is judged on both numbers in the run summary, not called an improvement on one alone. Click a player row to expand that player's detail: RPR components, ORtg (full) vs simple, TS%, GP, and the 3P% shrinkage applied.

Rookies: a rookie is a player in his first NBA season, from the ESPN roster entry (experience.years == 0). The Rookies section ranks them by RPR with the same 40% minutes floor, and lists notable below-floor rookies separately. The Rookies only button on the top-100 table filters it to rookies. Age, height, and weight in the CSVs also come from the roster pull; a player on no current roster has no experience or bio fields.

BUCK: best bang for your buck, on the BUCK page and as a stat on the Distributions page. BUCK is RPR divided by the average salary over the years left on the deal, in $M, including this season, so years left enter through the average, not through an invented weight. 0 is average production per dollar, positive is above average per dollar, negative is below average however cheap. The Top 25 uses the same 40% minutes floor as the leaderboards.

Best MVP: the best MVP season in history, on the Best MVP page. Every MVP winner ranked by combined PER, Win Shares, WS/48, BPM, and VORP from Basketball-Reference. The pick is Jordan 1987-88, ahead of LeBron 2008-09 and Jokic 2021-22. It is a historical page, separate from the 2026-27 ratings, and the daily update does not rebuild it.

Honest limits: about 15 games per team, so the order is still noisy. League-average prior only, no last-season prior, so the shrinkage pulls every player toward the same league average, rookies and veterans alike. Box-score only: no tracking, no on/off. RPR v1.2 adjusts ORtg for opponent defence; ORtg_full does not. Salaries on the team pages come from Basketball-Reference contracts and are display only: they do not change any rating or TradeScore, and a salary shown there does not prove a trade is legal. Teams reflect the 2026-27 rosters after a busy 2026 offseason: Giannis Antetokounmpo to Miami, LeBron James to Philadelphia, Walker Kessler to the Lakers, and Anthony Davis to Washington, among others. Full method in the README.

Data: ESPN public scoreboard and box scores. Code and full CSVs: RD3-LIL/nba-ratings on GitHub. Data through November 27, 2026 (271 games). Built November 28, 2026.
