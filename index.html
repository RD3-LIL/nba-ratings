Team needs and trade fit

Each team's strengths and weaknesses, its star, and the realistic players who would fill its biggest holes. Realistic is the point: targets are only Rotation / depth, Elite role player, Undervalued / buy-low, or High-value starter players, never a top-3 player on their own team, never a 20-point scorer or a 30-minute-a-night player on their own team. No contracts or salaries exist in the data, so none are used. This is a box-score fit, a prior for lineup data, not a lineup prediction. TradeScore is QualityGain plus FitGain minus the availability discount, on this run from 0.02 to 0.55, mostly FitGain; it is a fit ranking, not a prediction and not a pick. Each team page also lists less-common alternatives: its best targets who do not top many other teams' lists. Full per-team lists in team_needs_2026_27.csv.

Only 30 distinct players fill the 240 top-overall slots across the 30 teams: the same best available role players top many teams' lists (most often Paul Reed (28), Ajay Mitchell (20), Alex Caruso (19), Matisse Thybulle (19)). That is expected, the best available role players are the best fit for every needy team, but it means the lists read as nearly the same list. There is no asking-price or salary data to differentiate them.

How to read this

Getting around: the bar at the top jumps to the team ratings, the player leaders, the top 100 players, the rookies, or the GM board. East / West tabs above the team table filter to one conference; the filter box searches team names. Click a team name anywhere to open that team's own page, with its ratings, game log, and roster. Click a team row to see its game log and roster for the season right here. The RPR research page ranks the free data sources that could improve RPR, with the proposed formulas, the validation gate, and what is not worth adding.

AdjO / AdjD: points scored and allowed per 100 possessions, adjusted for opponent. The small number under each value is that team's rank in the metric; AdjD rank 1 is the best defense. Net is AdjO minus AdjD, and its rank is the Rk column. AdjT is adjusted tempo, possessions per game. Conf is East or West. W-L is the record in the games these ratings are built from, so W+L equals GP.

ORtg (players) is a simplified approximation: points per 100 possessions used, with no assist credit, so it underrates playmakers. Read it with %Poss. The tables show ORtg (full), the Oliver version with assist credit, falling back to shrunk simple ORtg where the full version is not available. RPR v1.2 is our own single-number player rating, not KenPom's proprietary kPOY: the ORtg that enters it is first shrunk toward the league-average ORtg (K = 200 possessions used) and adjusted for the defences the player faced (OppAdj, shrunk toward 0 with K = 500 possessions faced). On top of that, v1.2 recenters the counting terms (playmaking, defense, rebounding) on their league-average per-36 rates, so an average player scores about 0 from those terms and the median RPR is near 0, instead of every player getting free credit just for playing. The old values are kept as rpr_v1 and rpr_v1_1 in the CSVs. RPR_value = RPR v1.2 * GP / 82 is how much value a player has delivered so far; it is not part of the rating. Leaderboards only include players with at least 40% of their team's minutes, so a two-minute cameo cannot top the list. Click a player row to expand that player's detail: RPR components, ORtg (full) vs simple, TS%, GP, and the 3P% shrinkage applied.

Rookies: a rookie is a player in his first NBA season, from the ESPN roster entry (experience.years == 0). The Rookies section ranks them by RPR with the same 40% minutes floor, and lists notable below-floor rookies separately. The Rookies only button on the top-100 table filters it to rookies. Age, height, and weight in the CSVs also come from the roster pull; a player on no current roster has no experience or bio fields.

Honest limits: about 15 games per team, so the order is still noisy. League-average prior only, no last-season prior, so the shrinkage pulls every player toward the same league average, rookies and veterans alike. Box-score only: no tracking, no on/off. RPR v1.2 adjusts ORtg for opponent defence; ORtg_full does not. No contracts or salaries. Teams reflect the 2026-27 rosters after a busy 2026 offseason: Giannis Antetokounmpo to Miami, LeBron James to Philadelphia, Walker Kessler to the Lakers, and Anthony Davis to Washington, among others. Full method in the README.

Data: ESPN public scoreboard and box scores. Code and full CSVs: RD3-LIL/nba-ratings on GitHub. Data through November 19, 2026 (226 games). Built November 20, 2026.
