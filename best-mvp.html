Best MVP season ever

Every NBA MVP winner, ranked by stats and analytics. Regular season only, since that is what the MVP award covers. Numbers are Basketball-Reference, checked November 21, 2026.

Verdict: Michael Jordan, 1987-88.

35.0 points, 5.5 rebounds, 5.9 assists, 3.2 steals, and 1.6 blocks per game, in all 82 games. PER 31.7, Win Shares 21.2, WS/48 .308, BPM 12.95, VORP 12.46. He also won Defensive Player of the Year that same season. It is the only MVP season that is elite on every advanced stat at once, plus the defense to match.

How this is ranked

By combined dominance across five metrics, not by one stat alone:

  • PER: per-minute efficiency, league average is 15.
  • Win Shares (WS): total wins contributed over the season.
  • WS/48: wins contributed per minute.
  • BPM: points per 100 possessions above an average player.
  • VORP: total value above a replacement player.

Team record and two-way play are context, not the main driver. Playoffs are not counted in any of these numbers.

BPM and VORP note: Basketball-Reference has revised BPM/VORP since many older articles were written, so current leaderboard values are higher than the ones in older writeups. The numbers below use the current leaderboard throughout, so do not mix them with older articles. BPM and VORP do not exist before 1973-74, so Kareem, Wilt, and Oscar have no BPM/VORP in this comparison. That is a real gap, not a knock on them.

Why Jordan 1987-88 edges out No. 2 and No. 3

Versus LeBron 2008-09

The closest call, almost a tie. LeBron edges Jordan on BPM, 13.12 to 12.95, and on team wins, 66 to 50, and he finished second in Defensive Player of the Year voting. Jordan edges LeBron on total value, VORP 12.46 to 11.69 and WS 21.2 to 20.3, on scoring volume, 35.0 to 28.4, and on defense, he won Defensive Player of the Year outright with 3.2 steals per game. Jordan also played 82 games to LeBron's 81, at 40.4 minutes per game. If you weight team wins more, LeBron wins. If you weight individual two-way dominance over a full season, Jordan wins, and that is the tiebreaker used here.

Versus Jokic 2021-22

Jokic wins the rate stats. His PER 32.85 is No. 1 all time, and his BPM 13.63 is No. 3 all time on the current leaderboard. He was also the first player ever with 2,000 points, 1,000 rebounds, and 500 assists in a season, and he did it with Jamal Murray and Michael Porter Jr. mostly out. Where he loses is total value and defense. He played 74 games and 33.5 minutes per game, so his WS is only 15.2 and his VORP is 9.75, well below Jordan's 21.2 and 12.46. His team went 48-34 and lost in the first round. Jordan's season is dominant per minute and dominant in total. Jokic's is dominant per minute only.

Versus Curry 2015-16 and Jordan 1990-91

Curry has the best shooting season here, TS% .669, 402 threes, 50/40/90, unanimous MVP, and a 73-9 team. His BPM 11.87 and VORP 9.45 are a clear step below Jordan 1988, and his defense was good, not Defensive Player of the Year level. Jordan 1990-91 is actually the most balanced Jordan season after 1988, WS 20.3, WS/48 .321, VORP 10.77, plus a 61-21 title, but his scoring and steals are lower than 1988, and his BPM 12.01 is lower too.

Versus Kareem 1971-72

Kareem has the single biggest Win Shares outlier ever, 25.4, and WS/48 .340, both No. 1 all time, with 34.8 points and 16.6 rebounds per game. He ranks lower here because his PER 29.9 is below the top group, there is no BPM/VORP to cross-check him, and he played in a faster, smaller league. His team went 63-19 but lost in the conference finals to the 69-13 Lakers.

The Wilt correction

Wilt Chamberlain in 1961-62 did not win MVP, despite 50.4 points and 25.7 rebounds per game, PER 32.08, and WS 23.11. Bill Russell won it that year. Wilt also did not win in 1963-64 despite WS 25.0, second all time. Oscar Robertson won it. So Wilt 1961-62 is excluded from the ranking above because this page is MVP winners only. If you counted non-MVP seasons, 1961-62 would be in the top 3 conversation on raw stats alone.

Wilt's actual best MVP season is 1966-67: 24.1 points, 24.2 rebounds, and 7.8 assists, FG% .683, TS% .637, PER 26.5, WS 21.9, WS/48 .285, team 68-13 and a title. The WS total is elite, the PER is not, and there is no BPM/VORP or steals/blocks data to judge his defense properly.

Honorable mentions, and why they miss the top 10

  • Giannis Antetokounmpo 2019-20, Milwaukee 56-17: PER 31.9, No. 6 all time, BPM 11.45, TS% .613, 29.5 / 13.6 / 5.6, and MVP plus Defensive Player of the Year like Jordan 1988. He misses because he played only 63 games and 30.4 minutes per game in the shortened season, so WS is only 11.1 and VORP only 6.54. Per minute he belongs, in total he does not.
  • Oscar Robertson 1963-64, Cincinnati 55-25: 31.4 / 9.9 / 11.0, PER 27.6, TS% .576, WS 20.6, WS/48 .278, just 0.1 rebounds short of a triple-double average. Era pace inflates the raw line, and his PER/WS/48 are below the top group.
  • Larry Bird 1985-86, Boston 67-15: 25.8 / 9.8 / 6.8, PER 25.6, TS% .580, WS 15.8, WS/48 .244, BPM 8.74, VORP 8.42, title winner. Great team season, lower advanced peak than the top 10.
  • Magic Johnson 1986-87, LA Lakers 65-17: 23.9 / 6.3 / 12.2, PER 27.0, TS% .602, WS 15.9, WS/48 .263, BPM 8.84, VORP 7.97, title winner. Same story as Bird: brilliant playmaking, lower advanced peak.
  • Michael Jordan 1995-96, Chicago 72-10: 30.4 / 6.6 / 4.3, PER 29.4, TS% .582, WS 20.4, WS/48 .317, BPM 10.41, VORP 9.72, title winner. The best team season ever at the time, but Jordan's individual advanced numbers are below his 1988 and 1991 seasons.

Honest caveats

  • Era pace is huge. League pace was about 126.9 possessions per game in 1961-62, about 121.6 per game for the 1960s as a decade, about 109.4 in the 1970s, 102.2 in the 1980s, 94.6 in the 1990s, 92.1 in the 2000s, 95.3 in the 2010s, and 99.7 in the 2020s. Raw points, rebounds, and assists from the 1960s are inflated by 20 to 30 percent more possessions than modern seasons. PER, WS/48, and BPM are pace-adjusted in principle, but they still rely on box-score stats that were not tracked the same way, and they do not fully fix league size, talent pool, or rule changes.
  • Pre-1974 stats are incomplete. Steals and blocks were not tracked until 1973-74, and turnovers were not tracked until 1977-78. That means PER, WS, and especially BPM/VORP undercount defense and ball security for Wilt, Oscar, Kareem's early years, and Bill Russell. Russell is not in the top 10 here for that reason, even though his defense and winning are legendary.
  • PER loves volume scorers, BPM loves box-score stuffing. PER rewards high-usage scoring and does not capture off-ball defense or gravity well. BPM is a box-score regression, not tracking data. Curry's off-ball gravity and Jordan's on-ball defense are both undercounted. Use these as a combined check, not gospel.
  • MVP is regular season only. Curry's 73-9 team lost the Finals, Jordan's 50-32 team lost in the East semifinals, Jokic's 48-34 team lost in the first round, LeBron's 66-16 team lost in the East finals. If you want best season including playoffs, Jordan 1990-91, Shaq 1999-00, Bird 1985-86, Magic 1986-87, and Jordan 1995-96 all move up, and Curry and Jokic 2021-22 move down.
  • Current leaderboards include incomplete seasons. As of November 21, 2026, Basketball-Reference leaderboards list partial 2025-26 and 2026-27 seasons at the top of PER and BPM. Incomplete seasons are ignored here. Jokic 2024-25 PER 32.04 is complete, but he did not win MVP that year, Shai Gilgeous-Alexander did.
  • Close calls are real. Jordan 1988, LeBron 2009, and Jokic 2022 are separated by which metric you weight most. Change the weighting to rate-only and Jokic wins. Change it to team wins and LeBron 2009 or Curry 2016 wins. Change it to total Win Shares and Kareem wins by a lot. The No. 1 here is the best combined answer, not a unanimous one.
