# NBA Ratings (RonPom-style, for the NBA)

KenPom-style player and team ratings for the NBA, built the same way as
RonPom (College Ratings) but for the 2026-27 NBA season. Free ESPN data
only, no key, no paywall, no stats.nba.com in v1.

## Base ratings (same formulas as RonPom)

- Possessions used = FGA + 0.44 * FTA + TO.
- ORtg (approx) = 100 * PTS / possessions used. Simplified Dean Oliver:
  points produced divided by possessions used, with no assist credit
  and no offensive-rebound adjustment, so it underrates playmakers.
  Labelled approx in the output. Read it with %Poss.
- %Poss (usage) = Basketball-Reference usage formula:
  100 * (player poss used * (team MIN / 5)) / (player MIN * team poss used).
  20% is average, 25% a go-to guy.
- %Min = player MIN / (team games x 48). NBA games are 48 minutes,
  not 40. Slightly high in overtime games.
- RPR v1.2 (Nov 20, 2026) = our own single-number rating, in the
  spirit of KenPom's kPOY but a different, documented formula (kPOY
  is proprietary). The formula is unchanged from v1 in its weights,
  but v1.2 recenters the counting terms, which is what v1.1 got
  wrong: the scoring term was capped by the ORtg gap while AST-TO,
  STL+BLK, and REB were unbounded positives, so only 8 of 469
  players were negative and the median was +2.2. v1.2 centers each
  counting term on its league-average per-36 rate, so an average
  player scores about 0 from those terms:
  scoring = (ORtg_adj - league ORtg) * poss used per game / 100
  + playmaking = (0.5 * (AST36 - TO36) - 0.88) * MPG / 36
  + defense = ((STL36 + BLK36) - 1.98) * MPG / 36
  + rebounding = (0.25 * REB36 - 1.63) * MPG / 36.
  The centers (0.88, 1.98, 1.63 per 36 on the Nov 20 run) are
  minutes-weighted league averages from this season's data, not
  hand-set. Position-specific centers were tested and rejected: a
  rebound is worth the same whoever gets it, and centering by
  position erased the real difference between a center who
  rebounds and a guard who does not (it dropped Jokic to 8th).
  The scoring center is the season league ORtg on the player
  scale (100.25 on the Nov 20 run), not 100 and not the team-scale
  111.1; the two scales are never mixed. The weights (0.5, 1,
  0.25) are unchanged from v1: the fix is centering, not
  re-weighting. Result on this run: median -0.10, mean +0.01,
  56% negative, range -3.18 to +6.98. Split-half r (216 players):
  v1 0.480, v1.1 0.621, v1.2 0.542; team aggregation r: v1 0.718,
  v1.1 0.683, v1.2 0.747. v1.2's split-half is below v1.1's
  because v1.1's stability was partly the minutes-driven free
  credit (RPR correlated 0.64 with minutes share); team
  aggregation is the better check and v1.2 improves it. The old
  values are kept in parallel as rpr_v1 and rpr_v1_1 in the CSVs
  and JSON. Rough scale: a star is about +3 to +7, an average
  rotation player about -1 to +1, below average negative.
  What changed is the ORtg that enters it. ORtg is first shrunk
  toward the league-average ORtg:
  ORtg_shrunk = (PossUsed * ORtg + K * league ORtg) / (PossUsed + K),
  K = 200 possessions used. The prior is the league-average simple
  ORtg computed from this season's data (100.25 on the Nov 20 run);
  there is no prior season in this project, so rookies and veterans
  alike shrink to the same league average. K was calibrated by
  split-half: first half of each player's games predicting the
  second half (216 players), best K = 150 by MSE (207.5 vs 295.8
  unshrunk), curve flat from 150 to 250, so 200 is the midpoint,
  chosen for robustness. Then it is adjusted for the defences
  faced: OppAdj = possessions-weighted mean of (opponent AdjD -
  league-average AdjD) over the games the player played, where a
  player's possessions faced in a game = game possessions *
  (player minutes / team minutes in that game), AdjD from the same
  damped Gauss-Seidel solve as team_ratings.py. OppAdj is itself
  shrunk toward 0 with K = 500 possessions faced. ORtg_adj =
  ORtg_shrunk - OppAdj. On this data OppAdj is small (range -0.42
  to +0.49, mean ~0): a player's schedule is close to league
  average, so the adjustment is a nudge, not a shuffler. The big
  v1 to v1.1 moves are shrinkage: extreme small-sample ORtgs get
  pulled toward the average (e.g. Sam Hauser 135.7 -> 111.5,
  Draymond Green 54.2 -> 87.6).
- RPR_value = RPR v1.2 * GP / 82, reported separately ("how much
  value delivered so far"). It is never baked into the rating.
- TS% and 3P% are also shrunk toward league rates for display
  (ts_pct_shrunk, fg3_pct_shrunk): TS% m = 170 scoring attempts,
  3P% m = 750 attempts. They do not enter RPR.
- Leaderboards use a minutes floor: at least 40% of team minutes and
  at least 1 game, so a two-minute cameo cannot top the ORtg list.
- Team ratings: damped Gauss-Seidel solve (damping 0.5, prior weight
  300 possessions, league-average prior, home court estimated from
  the data), same as RonPom's solver.py. Home court is half the
  mean home-minus-away efficiency gap (0.50 on the Nov 20 run,
  from a 1.01 gap; home teams won exactly 113 of 226 games).
  Margins are capped at 20 points, the middle of a flat
  sensitivity range (cap 20 vs no cap moves ranks max 4, mean
  1.27; cap 15 max 6, mean 1.87; cap 25 max 4, mean 1.00; home
  0 vs 0.50 max 2, mean 0.47; home 1.5 max 2, mean 0.93; prior
  100 vs 300 max 1, mean 0.33). The build fails loudly if the
  solver does not converge. No D1 filtering: all 30 NBA teams
  are included. No prior from last season yet, so early-season
  team ratings lean on the league average.
- Per-36 is the NBA standard and is included alongside per-40
  (kept for comparability with RonPom).
- Two players changed teams mid-season in this data (Nique Clifford,
  OKC/Sacramento; Carson Cooper, Lakers/Memphis). They get one profile
  per team, as in RonPom.

## Evaluation (player_eval.py)

On top of the base ORtg / %Poss / RPR, per player:

- ORtg_full: a Dean Oliver / Basketball-Reference style offensive
  rating in points form (see `_oliver_ortg` in player_eval.py).
  Points produced = PTS, minus the FG credit transferred to passers
  (qAST), plus assist credit (2 x BR ASTPart), plus OREB credit
  (OREB x team OREB% x team points per possession). Possessions are
  the same as the simplified ORtg, so the two are directly comparable.
  Two deliberate choices: assist credit is scaled per team so total
  assist credit equals total FG debit (assists transfer points, they
  do not create them; team points produced ends up 2-5% above team
  points scored, the OREB bonus only), and an OREB is credited in
  points only, not also subtracted from possessions (that would
  double-count it). Kept next to ORtg_simple so the difference is
  visible. Jokic is the textbook case: ORtg_simple 102.6, ORtg_full
  108.7, because the simplified version gives him no assist credit.
- Shooting profile: TS%, eFG%, FG/3P/FT, plus points breakdown
  (points from 2s vs 3s vs FTs and their shares).
- Playmaking: AST/TO ratio, AST per 100 possessions used, AST per
  100 team possessions (assist-rate proxy).
- Defense/rebounding: STL+BLK per game and per 36, REB per 36 split
  OREB/DREB, PF per 36.
- Volume vs efficiency quadrant: %Poss vs ORtg_full. High usage is
  25%+, efficient is ORtg_full 115+. The high-usage efficient
  quadrant is the rare and valuable one.
- RPR component breakdown: scoring term, playmaking term, defense
  term, rebounding term. They sum to RPR, so any RPR can be explained.
- Per-36 for all counting stats.
- Consistency from game logs (computed from raw summaries, never
  invented): standard deviation of points per game, games with 20+
  and 30+ points.
- Minutes/durability: GP, %Min, MPG.
- Trending: last-7-days PPG vs season PPG (window Nov 13-19, 2026),
  with games in the window. Needs at least 3 games in the window to
  rank.

Top 10s printed by `python3 player_eval.py`: most efficient
high-usage, best playmakers, best defenders (STL+BLK per 36), best
rebounders (REB per 36), trending up, biggest ORtg lift from assist
credit.

Limits: box-score only. No tracking data, no on/off, no lineup data.
RPR v1.2 adjusts ORtg for opponent defence; ORtg_full does not, and
ORtg_full runs hot for low-usage players (a hyper-efficient bench
big on few possessions can post 130+), so always read it with %Poss;
the high-usage lens is the meaningful one. The shrinkage prior is
the league average for every player (no prior season in this
project), so a known elite shooter on a hot streak is still pulled
hard toward league average (e.g. Curry's 45.7% from three shrinks
to 36.5% at m = 750); the shrunk percentages are display-only and
do not enter RPR. Early season, about 15 games per team, so
everything is noisy.

## GM Board (gm_board.py)

A GM acquisition board built on the eval output. Every label is a
threshold rule in `gm_board.py`, never reputation.

Archetypes (first match wins as primary; all matches are listed):
primary scorer (%Poss >= 26 and PTS per 36 >= 24), playmaker
(AST per 36 >= 6 and AST/TO >= 2.0), rim protector (BLK per 36 >= 2
and REB per 36 >= 8), rebounder (REB per 36 >= 11 and PTS per 36 < 20),
defensive playmaker (STL per 36 >= 2 and AST per 36 >= 4), two-way big
(REB per 36 >= 8 and STL+BLK per 36 >= 2 and PTS per 36 >= 12),
3-and-D (%Poss < 22 and 3PA per 36 >= 5 and 3P% >= 0.36 and STL+BLK
per 36 >= 1.5 and AST per 36 < 4.5), specialist shooter (%Poss < 22
and 3P% >= 0.40 and 3PA per 36 >= 6), secondary scorer (%Poss 22-28
and PTS per 36 >= 18), bench scorer (PTS per 36 >= 20 and %Min < 50),
glue / role player (%Min >= 50 and RPR -0.5 to 1 and STL+BLK
per 36 >= 1). The usage gate on 3-and-D and specialist shooter
exists so a high-usage ball-handler is never labelled 3-and-D, and
the AST gate on 3-and-D does the same job from the other side
(Harden was labelled 3-and-D before it).

Acquisition tiers, checked in order. Role is judged by MPG when
the player plays, not by %Min: %Min conflates availability with
role, so a star who missed games (Curry, 34.5% Min at 26.5 MPG)
was labelled buy-low under the old %Min gates. Availability is a
red flag, not a tier.
- Insufficient minutes: GP under 8 or under 150 total minutes.
  Not tiered at all; this gate is checked first.
- Overvalued / avoid: high usage (%Poss >= 25) with ORtg_full < 100,
  or RPR < -1.0 at 20%+ usage, or 4+ turnovers per 36 with AST/TO
  < 1.2. The RPR bar is -1.0, not 0: in v1.2 about half the league
  is negative, so RPR < 0 alone is average, not overvalued. This
  guard is checked after the minutes gate and before the other
  tiers, so a high scorer with low efficiency and high turnovers
  cannot land in a top tier, and no low-GP player can be labelled
  Overvalued.
- Franchise cornerstone: RPR >= 3.5, ORtg_full >= 108, MPG >= 25,
  GP >= 10.
- All-Star level: RPR >= 2.5, ORtg_full >= 104, MPG >= 22.
- High-value starter: RPR >= 1.5, MPG >= 22, ORtg_full >= 100.
- Undervalued / buy-low: RPR >= 2.0 but MPG < 25, or RPR >= 1.5
  with ORtg_full >= 120 at under 22% usage.
- Elite role player: RPR 0.5-1.5, MPG >= 20.
- Rotation / depth: everything else.

Value signals: efficient at high usage (%Poss 25+ with ORtg_full
115+), two-way (STL+BLK per 36 >= 2 with PF per 36 <= 3.5),
availability (%Min 65+, GP 13+), shooting gravity (3PA per 36 >= 6,
3P% >= 0.37), playmaking (AST/TO >= 2), production per minute
(PTS per 36 >= 25), rebounding per minute (REB per 36 >= 11).

Red flags: high usage low efficiency, turnover prone (TO per 36 >= 4
or AST/TO < 1.0), foul prone (PF per 36 >= 4.5), low minutes /
availability risk (%Min < 40), small sample (GP < 8),
one-dimensional (scores 20+ per 36 with little rebounding, assists
or defense), early-season sample (under 13 games).

Fit lens (generic team needs, v1, not team-specific): shooting,
playmaking, rim protection, rebounding, perimeter defense, bench
scoring. `python3 gm_board.py --need shooting` returns the top 10
fits for a need (GP >= 8 required; shooting sorts by made 3s per 36,
not raw attempts).

Age, height (inches), and weight (lbs) in the GM board output
come from the roster pull (472 of 475 players; the 3 unmatched
are Tristan Enaruna, Dalen Terry, Lawson Lovering).

This run (RPR v1.2, Nov 20, 2026): 3 Franchise cornerstone, 9
All-Star level, 15 High-value starter, 3 Undervalued / buy-low,
37 Elite role player, 35 Overvalued / avoid, 188 Rotation / depth,
185 Insufficient minutes. Thresholds were recalibrated for the
v1.2 scale; the counts above are the generated numbers, see the
Current run section at the top of this README.

Limits: box-score only, no tracking, no on/off, early season (about
15 games per team). RPR here is v1.2 (recentered, shrunk,
opponent-adjusted). Tiers are a starting point for a target list,
not a trade machine.

## Team Needs and Trade Fit (team_needs.py)

For each of the 30 teams: strengths and weaknesses, the star, ranked
needs, and the *realistic* players who would fill them. Method follows
`research_notes/nba-team-needs-trade-fit-20261120-1902/report.md`
(Dean Oliver's Four Factors, Nylon Calculus lineup-balance work).

- **Diagnose in percentiles, never raw totals.** AdjO/AdjD plus the
  Four Factors on both ends (eFG%, TOV%, OREB%, FT rate, and the
  defensive mirrors: opp eFG%, opp TOV%, DREB%, opp FT rate), plus a
  minutes-weighted skill profile from the top 9 rotation players:
  shooting (made 3s per 36), playmaking (AST per 36), rebounding
  (REB per 36), rim protection (BLK per 36), perimeter defense
  (STL per 36 plus half-weight BLK per 36, a weak proxy: steals
  reward gambling and miss positional defense, flagged as such),
  bench scoring (PTS per 36 for players under 60% Min, ranked
  bench-only, and the team bench skill is the minutes-weighted
  mean over bench players in the rotation only, so starters
  scored 0 no longer drag it down). Strength is 65th percentile
  or better, weakness 35th or worse. Each team page shows this
  profile as a chart: horizontal percentile bars with a 60th
  percentile target line, orange below 60, blue at or above, with
  the raw per-36 rate behind each bar in the title text and
  listed under the chart.
- **Needs are star-anchored.** The star is the highest-RPR player
  with at least 8 games and 20 MPG when playing. Need = max(0, 60 -
  team skill percentile), weighted by base weights (shooting .25,
  playmaking .20, rebounding .15, rim protection .15, perimeter
  defense .15, bench scoring .10) times a star factor: 1.5 if the
  star is weak at that skill (35th pct or worse), 1.25 if below
  average, 0.5 if the star already supplies it (75th pct or better).
  A team whose point guard already facilitates gains little from
  another facilitator; a team whose star cannot defend needs a POA
  defender. Bench scoring is not a star skill (a starter's rate is
  0 by construction), so it never counts as a star gap.
- **A trade is scored as three numbers, not one.** The displaced
  player is the worst rotation player at the target's best skill,
  excluding the top 2 RPR players on the team (you do not bench the
  star or co-star for a role player), matched by athlete_id; a
  bench scorer displaces a bench player, not a starter.
  QualityGain = (RPR target - RPR displaced) x (target MPG / 240).
  FitGain = sum over skills of weight x (Need / 60) x
  sqrt(max(dSkill, 0) / 10), where dSkill is the minutes-weighted
  percentile change: concave, so the first rebounder or playmaker
  counts far more than the second. TradeScore = QualityGain +
  FitGain - availability discount. The usage penalty is gone: it
  was 0.0 for every target on the Nov 20 run, so it never bound
  and was removed rather than kept as decoration (the output field
  stays at 0.0 for backward compatibility). Availability discount
  is 10% of (QualityGain + FitGain) if GP under 10, plus 10% if
  %Min under 40. A few targets have a slightly negative
  QualityGain (minimum -0.02): worse on quality but positive on
  fit, and TradeScore stays positive via FitGain.
- **Realism / availability filter: no stars, ever.** Targets must be
  Rotation / depth, Elite role player, Undervalued / buy-low, or
  High-value starter; never Franchise cornerstone, All-Star level,
  or Overvalued / avoid. They must not be top-3 RPR on their own
  team, must play under 55% of team minutes and under 30 MPG, under
  26% usage, and under 20 PPG on their own team. Tier alone does not
  prove this: early-season noise puts big names in lower tiers (in
  this run LeBron James lands in Elite role player on RPR 0.69 and
  ORtg_full 97.5, and James Harden in Rotation / depth with a
  playmaker archetype), so the minutes / usage / scoring gates do
  the work. A target must fill a real need (FitGain > 0), must
  have RPR at or above 0.0 (league average in v1.2; the old 0.5
  floor was set on the v1.1 scale and left only 10 eligible
  targets league-wide), and the RPR jump over the displaced
  player is capped at 5.0. This run: 240 top targets across 30
  teams, 8 per team, and no suggested target is a Franchise
  cornerstone or All-Star player. There are no unfilled needs on
  this run (0, down from Detroit rebounding in the v1.1 run).
  "Realistic" is a data-only proxy for availability, never a claim
  a player is actually available.
- **Star clarity and the 2026 offseason.** Teams reflect the
  2026-27 rosters after a busy 2026 offseason: Giannis
  Antetokounmpo to Miami, LeBron James to Philadelphia, Walker
  Kessler to the Lakers, and Anthony Davis to Washington, among
  others, so 2026-27 teams look different from 2025-26. The site
  says so on the index and every team page. A team whose
  highest-RPR qualifying player is under RPR 1.5 is labelled
  "No clear star in this data" instead of naming a role player
  as the star (eight teams in this run: Boston, Minnesota,
  Orlando, Milwaukee, Charlotte, Toronto, Indiana, LA Clippers).
  Those are rebuilding teams that traded their star away,
  Milwaukee after trading Giannis, not data errors. 1.5 is about
  the 85th percentile among qualified players and sits in a
  natural gap in the team-top distribution.
- **Calibration honesty.** The per-skill FitGain cap (2.0) is a
  tiebreaker and safety rail, not a fitted effect. On this run
  the largest per-skill FitGain contribution is 0.164 and the
  theoretical maximum is about 0.4, so the cap never binds on
  real data. It only separates near-tied targets. v2 should fit
  it against post-trade Adj Net.
- **The lists barely differentiate.** Only 30 distinct players
  fill the 240 top-overall slots; Paul Reed tops 18 teams' lists
  as #1, and across the top 8 slots Paul Reed appears on 28
  teams, Ajay Mitchell on 20, Alex Caruso and Matisse Thybulle
  on 19 each. That is expected, the best available role
  players are the best fit for every needy team, but the
  per-team lists read as nearly the same list. There is no
  asking-price or salary data to differentiate them; a cap on
  how many teams one player can top was considered and rejected
  because it would hide the true best fit for the later teams.
  Each team page also lists less-common alternatives: its best
  targets who top at most 2 other teams' lists as #1, so a
  reader can see a fit that is not the same name everywhere.

## Rookies (fetch_rosters.py)

A rookie is a player in his first NBA season. The rule is exact
and comes from the ESPN roster endpoint, not from memory:
`is_rookie` is True exactly when the roster entry shows
`experience.years == 0`. `fetch_rosters.py` pulls all 30 rosters
(528 athletes, cached per team under `data/raw/roster/`) and
writes `data/rosters_2026_27.json`. `player_profiles.py` merges
it by athlete_id, never by name alone, adding `is_rookie`,
`experience_years`, `age`, `height_in`, and `weight_lbs` to the
base profiles and CSV; `player_eval.py` carries `is_rookie` and
`experience_years` into the eval CSV and JSON.

Verified against the live pull: the rule returns the 2026 draft
class (AJ Dybantsa, Cameron Boozer, Darryn Peterson, Nate Ament,
Koa Peat, Labaron Philon Jr., Darius Acuff Jr., Mikel Brown Jr.,
Aday Mara, Thomas Sorber, Yaxel Lendeborg, and the rest), 75
rookies on rosters, 58 of whom have played (57 distinct athletes:
Carson Cooper has one profile per team after changing teams, and
17 roster rookies have not played yet). The 2025 draft class are
second-year players in 2026-27, not rookies: Cooper Flagg and
Cedric Coward both show `experience.years == 2`. No veteran with
5+ years is in the rookie list.

Current best rookie by RPR v1.2 (Nov 20, 2026, min 2 games and 40%
of team minutes, same floor as every leaderboard): Aday Mara
(Oklahoma City Thunder), RPR 0.77, 9.2 PPG in 15 games. Next are
Cameron Boozer (0.17), Meleek Thomas (-0.14), Brayden Burries
(-0.22), and Yaxel Lendeborg (-0.31). (In v1.1 Boozer led at 4.04;
v1.2's recentering pulls every high-minutes player down by the
league-average counting credit, about 3.7 for a 30 MPG player, so
the rookie order compresses and Mara, on fewer minutes, leads.)
The raw RPR leader among below-floor rookies is Tobe Awaka (1.44
in v1.2), but on only 4 games at 6.0% of minutes, so he sits in
the below-floor list, labelled as a small sample, not at the top
of the ranking. The site has a Rookies section (top 10 by RPR
plus notable below-floor rookies) and a Rookies only toggle on
the top-100 players table.

Ambiguous cases, handled conservatively: Johni Broome's ESPN
roster entry carries no experience value, so he is not labelled
a rookie (only an explicit 0 counts). Three players in the box
scores are on no current roster (Tristan Enaruna, Dalen Terry,
Lawson Lovering) and likewise have no experience or bio fields.
One rookie's roster team differs from his box-score team (Carson
Cooper: roster says Lakers, box scores say Memphis, where he
played); the roster never overrides the box-score team. Rosters
are fetched once per season by `fetch_rosters.py`, and re-fetched
weekly by `daily_update.sh` (if the roster file is older than 7
days it clears the raw roster cache and re-pulls).

## Known gaps (not invented)

- Contracts and salaries: there is no free source. Spotrac and
  similar are paywalled and are not scraped. Any trade-value or
  salary-cap work needs a contract source added first.
- No tracking or on/off data in v1.

## Site (build_page.py, RonPom-style)

`python3 build_page.py` writes `index.html` at the repo root plus
one page per team under `teams/` (30 pages), from the ratings
files above. No network, stdlib only.

- **Team ratings table**: all 30 teams, rank / team / conf / W-L /
  AdjO / AdjD / Net / AdjT. The small number under each AdjO, AdjD,
  and AdjT value is that team's rank in the metric (1 is best;
  AdjD rank 1 is the best defense). Net rank is the Rk column.
  Click a column header to sort, use the filter box to search team
  names, and use the East / West tabs to filter by conference.
  Conf is East or West from a fixed map of the standard NBA
  alignment (ESPN's NBA teams endpoint carries no conference
  field). W-L is the record in the games the ratings are built
  from, so W+L equals GP.
- **Click a team row** to expand its game log and roster inline;
  **click a team name** to open that team's own page
  (`teams/<slug>.html`) with its ratings, game log, and roster.
  Rosters are sorted by minutes and carry each player's GM board
  tier. Game logs list date, opponent, venue (H/A/N), result, and
  score, in date order.
- **Player leaders**: top 5 by PTS, REB, AST, ORtg, and RPR.
  Same floor as RonPom's 2026-27 leaders: min 2 games and 40% of
  team minutes.
- **Top players**: top 100 by RPR v1.2, sortable and filterable,
  team names link to team pages. The ORtg column is ORtg (full),
  the Oliver version with assist credit, falling back to shrunk
  simple ORtg where the full version is not available. Click a
  player row to expand that player's detail: RPR components,
  ORtg (full) vs simple, TS%, GP, and the 3P% shrinkage applied.
  Sorting renumbers the Rk column. A Rookies only button filters
  the table to first-year players.
- **Rookies**: top 10 rookies by RPR (same min 2 games and 40%
  minutes floor), plus notable rookies below the floor, labelled
  as small samples. Best rookie so far: Aday Mara, RPR 0.77.
- **GM board**: acquisition tiers, same as `gm_board.py`.
- **Team needs**: one row per team with its star, top need, and top
  realistic target with QualityGain / FitGain / TradeScore, plus the
  needs with no realistic target and why. Team pages carry the same
  block (strengths, weaknesses, top 3 needs, top 3 targets, plus
  less-common alternatives and the raw per-36 rates behind the
  skill chart).
- **Data through**: the header of the index and every team page
  shows "Data through November 19, 2026 (226 games)", read from
  the games file by the builder, never hardcoded.

## How to re-run daily

```bash
cd ~/workspace/nba-ratings
./daily_update.sh            # fetch last 3 days, rebuild everything, push (waits for approval tap)
./daily_update.sh --no-push  # rebuild locally only, no push
DAILY_UPDATE_PUSH=0 ./daily_update.sh
```

`daily_update.sh` mirrors RonPom's daily updater: it re-fetches
the last 3 days of scoreboards AND summaries fresh (raw summaries
for the window's events are deleted first, so late box-score
corrections land), re-fetches rosters weekly if the roster file
is older than 7 days, exits without doing anything if no new
games arrived and rosters did not change, otherwise rebuilds
player profiles, team ratings, eval, GM board, and team needs,
runs the RPR validator (stops on a failed gate), regenerates the
run summary and README numbers, rebuilds the site, then prepares
a GitHub push manifest (the rebuilt CSVs and JSONs, validation
and run-summary outputs, `README.md`, `rpr-research.html`,
`index.html`, and all 30 team pages) and pushes it, waiting for
Ron's approval tap. A sync-base check stops the push if the
branch head moved since the last sync. No cron is set up yet; the
script is the 6:00 AM Chicago pattern, ready to schedule.

Manual rebuild (same steps the script runs):

```bash
cd ~/workspace/nba-ratings
python3 fetch_nba.py --season 2026-27   # resumes per date; season end is 2027-04-11 in fetch_nba.py
python3 fetch_rosters.py                # weekly by the daily update, not daily
python3 player_profiles.py
python3 team_ratings.py
python3 player_eval.py
python3 gm_board.py
python3 team_needs.py
python3 validate_rpr_v11.py             # exits 1 on a failed gate
python3 generate_run_summary.py         # regenerates data/run_summary.json and the README numbers
python3 build_page.py
```

`daily_update.sh` runs the same chain except the roster pull
(rosters change slowly, so they are re-fetched weekly, not
daily). Its push manifest includes the rebuilt CSVs and JSONs,
`data/validation.json`, `data/run_summary.json`,
`data/rosters_2026_27.json`, `data/fetch_state.json`, `README.md`,
`rpr-research.html`, `index.html`, and all 30 team pages.

## v2 ideas (not built)

- `nba_api` / stats.nba.com for advanced stats: PER, Box Plus/Minus,
  Win Shares, tracking (speed, distance, touches), shot charts, on/off.
  It is free with no key, but it rate-limits aggressively and blocks
  some cloud IPs, so it would be a second source, not a replacement
  for the ESPN fetch. The ESPN pipeline stays the reproducible base.
- Last-season prior for team ratings (like RonPom's noshrink prior).
- Team needs v2: lineup net ratings and RAPM / EPM / LEBRON as the
  quality input instead of RPR, shot-location data, tracking for real
  gravity and rim deterrence, and learned archetype-interaction
  coefficients instead of the hand-set need weights and usage
  penalty. Back-test TradeScore against actual post-trade Adj Net
  changes; if it does not beat RPR difference alone, the fit term
  is decoration and should say so.
