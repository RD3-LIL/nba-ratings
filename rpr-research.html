How to improve RPR: free data, ranked

Research read 20 November 2026, for the 2026-27 season (about 15 games per team). The rule throughout: only add a source if the benefit clearly outweighs the cost, complexity, fragility, and noise. ← Back to NBA Ratings

Snapshot, not updated daily. This page was written 20 Nov 2026 and is not rebuilt by the daily updater. The current formula is RPR v1.2 (see the "Where RPR stands now" section below, updated 28 Nov 2026): defense full-weighted, counting terms centered, no minutes shrink, no minutes floor. RPR v1.3 (defense half-weighted, counting terms shrunk, OppAdj K = 150, no RPR under 5 games or 50 total minutes) is kept in parallel in the data, not the headline. The validation table in the "Where this project is" section is the Nov 20 run; the current run's numbers are in the README run summary. The source rankings above are still the research as read on 20 Nov 2026.

Short answer: do not buy or bolt on a black-box metric. The worthwhile work is (1) shrinkage and opponent adjustment using data the project already has, (2) one careful, cached pull of NBA tracking, hustle, and shot-location data via nba_api, and (3) using free, pre-built RAPM / impact files (SportsDataverse nba_player_impact, DARKO, OpenRAPM) as a prior and validation benchmark, not as a daily ingredient.

The single biggest RPR blind spot is defence, and the only honest fix at 15 games is a small, heavily shrunk defence term from a prior-informed impact metric, not raw single-season RAPM, not raw on/off, and not a full EPM / LEBRON import. At 15 games every impact estimate is mostly prior; pretending otherwise is the main way this project would get worse while looking more sophisticated.

Where RPR stands now

The current formula (RPR v1.2, headline since 28 Nov 2026; v1.3 is kept in parallel, see the section at the bottom):

RPR v1.2 = (ORtg_adj − league ORtg) × PossUsed/G / 100
    + (0.5 × (AST36 − TO36) − 0.88) × MPG / 36
    + ((STL36 + BLK36) − 1.98) × MPG / 36
    + (0.25 × REB36 − 1.63) × MPG / 36

ORtg_adj is the simple ORtg (points per 100 possessions used, no assist credit), first shrunk toward the league-average ORtg (K = 200 possessions used) and then adjusted for the defences faced (OppAdj, shrunk toward 0 with K = 150 possessions faced). K = 500 was for a full season and crushed the adjustment to about 0.1 points in November, when players have faced only about 100-230 possessions; K = 150 is re-checked at mid-season. The fuller Oliver ORtg with assist credit is a separate column, ORtg_full, shown on the site next to the simple ORtg; it does not enter RPR. The counting terms are centered on their minutes-weighted league-average per-36 rates (0.88, 1.98, 1.63 on the Nov 20 run), so an average player scores about 0 from them. Defense is full-weighted in v1.2: v1.3 half-weighted it because at full weight it was 46% of Wembanyama's RPR and put Dyson Daniels 9th on steals alone, but that change was rejected on 26 Nov 2026 (defense is important) and the headline reverted to v1.2 on 28 Nov 2026. PossUsed/G is possessions used per game. Verified weaknesses: defence is STL+BLK only (misses positional defence, rim deterrence, point-of-attack defence); playmaking is AST−TO only (misses creation gravity and spacing); no on/off; no tracking; no shot location; per-game volume leans on minutes; and about 15-game samples are noisy. Opponent adjustment at player level now exists (it did not in v1); the minutes correlation is gone as a free-credit problem, because the centering removes it. Tradeoff, stated honestly: v1.2 split-half was 0.625 on the Nov 22 run, below v1.1's 0.691, while its team aggregation was 0.729 vs 0.67, so v1.2 was more honest at team level and less stable player to player; v1.3, kept in parallel, is judged on both numbers in the run summary, not called an improvement on one alone.

Formula changes in the proposals below are labelled PROPOSAL where a coefficient is a choice, not a fact. Coefficients must be calibrated by the validation protocol in the Validation section, not taken as sourced facts.

What each free source adds

stats.nba.com via nba_api (Python) - highest leverage

MIT-licensed Python wrapper around the undocumented JSON endpoints that power NBA.com stats, 130+ endpoints, no API key, no signup, $0. pip install nba_api. No documented rate limits, which is the problem: stats.nba.com throttles rapid requests and does not respond to many cloud-hosting IP ranges (AWS, GCP, Azure, Colab, and others). Independent testing in Nov 2026 found hustle and tracking-defence endpoints timing out after 45–90 s on a single request with a descriptive user agent and no browser headers. Practical rules: pause between requests, send full browser headers (Referer, User-Agent, x-nba-stats-origin), cache everything, fetch league-wide dashboards (one call per stat family per day) rather than one call per player. Reliability: medium-low as a daily dependency; endpoints break when the NBA changes headers/schemas, and some silently return empty responses. Mitigation: cache daily, keep ESPN as fallback, never let a failed tracking pull block the RPR update.

Fields that matter, league-wide dashboards, one call each: LeagueDashPtStats (passing: passes, potential assists, assist points created, secondary assists; drives, touches, rebounding, shooting by dribbles / touch-time / defender distance, speed/distance), LeagueDashPtDefend / PlayerDashPtShotDefend (opponent FG% vs. baseline, defended attempts, by zone), LeagueHustleStatsPlayer (deflections, contested shots, loose balls recovered, charges drawn, screen assists, box-outs), ShotChartDetail (every shot with x/y coordinates, zone, shot type, defender distance), LeagueDashLineups (5-man lineup Net/Off/Def rating and possessions), and LeagueDashPlayerStats with MeasureType=Defense/Advanced/Misc.

Basketball-Reference advanced stats (BPM, VORP, PER, Win Shares)

Free to browse, no official API. Access is HTML scraping, or community scrapers. $0 to view; Stathead (the query/export layer) is paid and is not needed here. Sports Reference Terms of Use govern use, with a linked data-use and bot-traffic/limiting policy: respect robots.txt, ≥3 s delays, cache, identify with a User-Agent, do not harm site performance, do not use for profit or a competing product. Reliability: high for the numbers themselves, low as a pipeline (HTML structure changes, blocks happen, no contract). Fields: TS%, eFG%, ORtg/DRtg, usage, PER, BPM (OBPM/DBPM), VORP, Win Shares/48, per-100 stats, shooting splits by zone, on/off. What it adds: almost nothing RPR does not already have. BPM 2.0 is a box-score regression trained to predict RAPM, a linear combination of the same box-score inputs RPR already uses. Its proper role is validation benchmark, not ingredient.

Dunks and Threes - EPM

Per-100-possession impact metric (O-EPM / D-EPM / EPM), updated nightly, covering every day since 2001–02. Two components: a statistical plus-minus (SPM) model built on "Estimated Skills", and a RAPM calculation. Web leaderboards are free to view; programmatic access is a token-authenticated API (/api/v1/epm, /api/v1/epm-all, /api/v1/season-epm). Cost not verified as free: the API requires a token, the site gates custom-date team ratings and other views behind subscription, and no subscription/API price was found in searches on 20 Nov 2026. Do not budget $0, and do not assume the token is free. API v1 is legacy and will be retired at the end of the 2026–27 season; new integrations should use v2 (/api/v2/epm-actual, /api/v2/team-epm). Responses support ETag / If-None-Match and gzip (4–8× smaller). Since 19 Oct 2026, season EPM is padded toward the player's preseason Expected EPM rather than replacement level. What it adds: the single best off-the-shelf defence term available (D-EPM) plus a validated overall impact number, but it is a black box whose SPM coefficients and RAPM prior are not public in full. Importing EPM wholesale would make RPR "EPM, plus some box score" and destroy the transparency that is RPR's reason to exist. Validation: prior-season EPM game-margin R² = 0.157 (90% CI .141–.174), team win% r = .778 in hoop-impact-bench, good, but not separated from LEBRON (.159), DARKO (.155), or BPM (.154) once bootstrap intervals are considered. Note: "DunkAPI" at dunkapi.com (500 free credits/month, $19/mo starter) is a different, unrelated product; do not confuse the two.

BBall-Index - LEBRON

Luck-adjusted player Estimate using a Box prior Regularized ON-off. Box-score prior (boxPIPM weights, stabilised by offensive archetype) + luck-adjusted RAPM. Reported as LEBRON / O-LEBRON / D-LEBRON (per 100 poss) and LEBRON WAR. Free interactive dashboards; the site lists seven free tools including the LEBRON Suite. No free bulk CSV download or public API was found. Full access is $5/month or $52.50/year. The 700+ talent grades (perimeter defence, interior defence, playmaking, screening, etc.) are the deeper product, but they are also behind the paywall/app. A daily pipeline would require either manual dashboard reads or scraping an app; neither is a supported feed. What it adds: D-LEBRON is a credible defence term; the talent grades are the only source in this report that directly measures who a player guards and how hard that assignment is, but that is exactly the part that is not freely exportable. Validation: prior-season LEBRON game-margin R² = 0.159 (.142–.175), team win% r = .770, statistically indistinguishable from EPM / BPM / DARKO.

nbarapm.com - pure and factor RAPM

Free site publishing RAPM (ridge regression of possession outcomes on who was on the floor, no box score at all) in rolling windows (2/3/4/5-year, time-decay) and a six-factor decomposition: RAPM = oTS + oTOV + oREB + dTS + dTOV + dREB. Free website, per-player pages and leaderboards. No bulk CSV/API download URL was verified on 20 Nov 2026; the datasets page is rendered in-page. Treat as "free to read, not free to ingest" unless a download link is found on the site itself. Cost $0 to view. Reliability medium: a one-person / community site; update cadence and long-term availability are not guaranteed. What it adds: the cleanest defence signal conceptually (DRAPM sees rim deterrence, point-of-attack defence, help, everything the box score misses) and a factor split that tells you how a player helps. But at 15 games a 1-year RAPM is mostly noise, and the multi-year versions that are stable are mostly last season's information. Stability evidence: single-season RAPM year-over-year rank repeatability is Spearman 0.322 (2024–25 to 2025–26, 293 players with >2,000 possessions, SportsDataverse docs). The consensus is "never trust one season of RAPM; use multi-year and combine with box-score metrics".

Cleaning the Glass

Ben Falk's site: lineup and on/off stats with shot-by-location and play-context splits, garbage-time filtering, position-adjusted percentiles. Website only, no API. Most of the useful content is subscriber-only: $5/month after a 1-week free trial. High editorially, unusable as a pipeline: no export, no API, paywall. Lineups shown only with ≥15 possessions; percentiles vs. lineups with ≥100 possessions. What it adds: nothing RPR cannot get from stats.nba.com lineups plus its own play-by-play parsing, except CTG's cleaning (garbage-time removal, shot-location splits).

pbpstats / hoopR play-by-play

Two different things share the name. pbpstats (Python package): open-source parser that scrapes play-by-play from stats.nba.com / data.nba.com, adds the lineup on the floor for every event, and splits games into possessions. Free, MIT-style open source. It inherits stats.nba.com fragility and needs one request per game (about 1,230 games/season), so a daily refresh is heavy; a cached, incremental build is the only sane mode. pbpstats.com / PBP Stats API: the hosted API for the same derived possession/lineup/on-off/WOWY data. Paid/subscriber (no public price list found). Do not assume the API is free because the parser is. hoopR (R package, CRAN): wraps ESPN, NBA Stats API, Basketball-Reference, and others. Its killer feature is not the live wrappers but the pre-built release loaders (load_nba_pbp(), load_nba_shots(), load_nba_stats_possessions(), load_nba_player_impact()) that pull whole seasons as CSV / Parquet / RDS from GitHub releases, "fast, no rate limits, NBA from 2002". The nba_*() live wrappers hit stats.nba.com and are "slow and sometimes blocked from cloud servers". hoopR is R; if the project is Python, use sportsdataverse-py instead (same data releases). What it adds: possession-level truth, the precondition for on/off, RAPM, opponent adjustment, and shot-quality work. But building and maintaining a possession engine is a project, not a data source; only take the pre-built releases, not the live scrape.

Shot-location / tracking data

There is no separate "shot location API"; it is three views of the same underlying data. ShotChartDetail via nba_api: every FGA with x/y (tenths of a foot, origin at hoop), zone, shot type, defender distance, shot-clock; $0, throttled. load_nba_shots() / load_nba_stats_shots() via hoopR / sportsdataverse-py: same shots, pre-compiled per season from GitHub releases; $0, no rate limit. ESPN PBP via hoopR load_nba_pbp(): shot coordinates when ESPN has them, plus play text; $0. Hawk-Eye / Second Spectrum raw tracking (player x/y 25×/s) is not public; do not plan around it. DunkAPI (dunkapi.com): REST/MCP, API key, 500 free credits/month, then $19/mo (10k credits); play-by-play 10 credits, shot chart 10 credits; every shot located since 2004 plus expected_points per shot. What it adds: shot diet (rim/mid/3 mix), shot quality (expected eFG% from location), and shot-making over expected (eFG% − xeFG%). This separates "good shooter" from "good shot-taker" and "lucky shooter", the single most actionable offensive refinement, because shooting percentages at 15 games are mostly noise.

On/off and lineup data

Free routes: stats.nba.com LeagueDashLineups + team/player dashboards (via nba_api); hoopR/sportsdataverse load_nba_stats_lineups() release files; ESPN PBP + pbpstats parser (build your own); CTG ($5/mo). What it adds: on/off is the raw material of RAPM, not a rating itself. Raw on/off at 15 games is dominated by teammates, opponent, garbage time, and shooting luck; the project already has team AdjO/AdjD, so a naive on/off term would mostly re-add team strength with noise. Its correct uses are (a) diagnostic, flag players whose RPR and on/off disagree violently, and (b) input to a regularised model (RAPM), never a direct additive term at full weight. A single player at 15 games has roughly 300–500 on-court possessions, an order of magnitude below what RAPM needs to separate players.

Other free APIs / datasets

SportsDataverse pre-built player-impact files (best free RAPM route). GitHub releases, nba_player_impact_{season}.parquet, one row per player-team-season since 1997 with o_rapm / d_rapm / rapm, o_adj_rapm / d_adj_rapm / adj_rapm (RAPM shrunk toward the previous season's SPM instead of zero), SPM, BPM 2.0, WAR, and a DARKO-style Kalman projection (boban_filtered_skill, boban_projected_rating, boban_projected_sd). Fixed ridge penalty (2,683) so seasons compare; regular-season RAPM SD 1.53–1.99 per 100. Also nba_stats_possessions (every possession with all 10 players) and nba_stats_rolling_windows. Loaders: hoopR::load_nba_player_impact() (R) or sportsdataverse-py (Python). Cost $0, no key, no rate limit (GitHub release download). Caveat: season-level files built from a live stats.nba.com scrape ("run from a residential IP, stats.nba.com hangs on cloud IPs"), so check how current the 2026–27 file is before treating it as daily data.

DARKO (darko.app). Free leaderboard with CSV downloads (leaderboard, team, compare, standings, WOWY, lineups, fantasy lab); no account mentioned for downloads. DPM is Bayesian (exponential decay + Kalman filter), inputs are box scores + tracking + BRef/NBA.com + pbpstats, coverage since 1996–97; Box DPM (box only) vs DPM (adds on-off). Published example from crawls: Jokic +7.1 DPM, Wembanyama +6.9 (Nov 2026). No documented bulk API; an MCP endpoint exists for AI assistants, not a data feed, so do not build a pipeline on it.

OpenRAPM (openrapm.com). Prior-informed RAPM by Nathan Walker (ex-GSW data science). Free site; bundled MIT-licensed snapshot in hoop-impact-bench. Prior-season OpenRAPM game-margin R² = 0.162 (.146–.178), top-3 among public metrics.

FiveThirtyEight RAPTOR (archival). modern_RAPTOR_by_player.csv / historical_RAPTOR_by_player.csv (CC BY 4.0) via fivethirtyeight/data on GitHub. Stopped in 2023: useful for backtesting on past seasons, useless for 2026–27.

ESPN undocumented API (site.api.espn.com). No auth, JSON, scoreboard/summary (box score + PBP + win probability). The project already uses ESPN; this adds PBP and shot coordinates in some responses, but it is undocumented, no SLA, may change without notice, and adds little over nba_api tracking.

balldontlie API. Free tier covers teams/players/games; statistics, play-by-play, lineups, and injuries require paid tiers. Not recommended: a reseller of the same data, with the good parts paid.

Kaggle NBA datasets. Community uploads; provenance, licence, coverage, and correction behaviour must be checked per dataset. Stale by construction. Only for backtests, never for a live rating.

Proposed formula changes

Notation: Poss/G = possessions played per game (about MIN/G × team pace / 48); PossUsed/G = possessions used (scoring attempts + turnovers + assist credit, Oliver sense). Any per-100-possession impact term must be multiplied by Poss/G / 100 to become points-per-game (RPR's unit), and any term that re-uses box-score inputs already in RPR must be netted against the term it replaces, or it double-counts.

Shrinkage: the baseline that costs nothing (do this before adding any source)

At 15 games, raw ORtg and raw shooting percentages are mostly noise. Shrink every rate toward a prior before it enters RPR:

ORtg_shrunk  = (PossUsed × ORtg + K × ORtg_prior) / (PossUsed + K)
  ORtg_prior = player's prior-season ORtg (or 110, league average,
                for rookies / no history)
  K ≈ 150–250 possessions used  [PROPOSAL, calibrate by split-half]

TS%_shrunk, 3P%_shrunk:  (Makes + m × LeagueRate) / (Attempts + m)
  m ≈ 170 for TS% (stabilises ~169 attempts),
  m ≈ 750 for 3P% at reliability 0.7,
  or m ≈ 480 at reliability ~0.5
  At 15 games (typically 60–90 3PA) a player's raw 3P% should be
  trusted at well under half weight.

Evidence: split-half analysis finds FT% stabilises at ~27 attempts, TS% at ~169, and 3P% "never reached 0.5 [reliability] within the samples real players have" up to 480 attempts; shrinkage cuts next-season prediction error by 53% for 3P% and 32% for FT% (CourtLens). The older KR-21 estimate is ~750 3PA for reliability 0.7, more than a full season's record for most players. Implication: never let a 15-game hot/cold shooting stretch move RPR by its full raw amount. Also shrink RPR itself toward a prior for low-minute players, and report a minutes floor: no full RPR rank for players under ~200 possessions played; show them as "provisional".

Opponent adjustment (uses existing team AdjO/AdjD, no new source)

OppAdj = weighted mean of (opponent AdjD − league-average AdjD)
         over the player's minutes, weighted by possessions
         faced vs. each opponent
ORtg_adj = ORtg_shrunk − OppAdj        [PROPOSAL: 1-for-1 subtraction]
  Scoring 115 per 100 against defences that are on average 3 points
  better than league average is credited as ~118.
DefOppAdj (for the defence term): add back the quality of opposing
  offences faced, symmetrically, using opponent AdjO.

Cost: one join of game logs to team AdjD. Fragility: zero new vendors. This is the highest benefit/cost change in the whole report and should be done before any external metric is considered. Caveat: team AdjD is itself estimated from the same ~15 games, so shrink OppAdj by the same logic (weight by possessions faced; shrink toward 0 with K ≈ 500 team-possessions faced).

Defence term, from D-EPM / DRAPM / D-LEBRON / DARKO-defence (the main prize)

Replace, do not stack, the STL+BLK term. STL+BLK is a subset of what D-EPM/DRAPM measures (contests, deterrence, point-of-attack defence, help, rebounding value). Keeping both at full weight double-counts steals/blocks.

DefPts/G = D_impact_shrunk × Poss/G / 100
  D_impact_shrunk = λ × D_metric + (1 − λ) × D_box_prior
  D_metric  = D-EPM (preferred: tracking SPM + RAPM, nightly,
              season-padded) or DRAPM from a multi-year window,
              or D-LEBRON, or DARKO defence
  D_box_prior = a box-score defence prior, e.g. standardised
                (STL+BLK)/Poss, keeps the term defined when the
                external metric is missing
  λ = 0.25 at ~15 games, rising to 0.50 at a full season  [PROPOSAL]
  λ_eff = λ × Poss / (Poss + 800)  [PROPOSAL]

RPR_v2 = (ORtg_adj − 100) × PossUsed/G / 100
       + PlayTerm/G
       + DefPts/G                         (replaces (STL+BLK)/G)
       + 0.25 × REB/G                     (see rebound note)

Worked scale check (arithmetic only, no player data invented): a player with D-EPM +2.0 playing 30 min on a 100-possession-pace team plays ~62 possessions; DefPts/G = 2.0 × 0.62 = +1.24 points/game. That is the same order as (STL+BLK)/G for a good defender (~2.0), so the term does not swamp RPR, but at λ = 0.25 the incremental move vs. the old term is ~±0.3–0.6 pts/game for most players, which is honest at this sample. If the defence term moves RPR by more than that at 15 games, the weight is too high.

Which D-metric? D-EPM first (nightly, season-padded toward preseason Expected EPM since Oct 2026, O/D split published). Multi-year DRAPM second (stable, but stale: it rates last season more than this one). D-LEBRON third (no bulk access). Never use single-season RAPM at λ > 0.25, and never use raw on/off as the D-metric.

Rebound note: keep 0.25 × REB/G only if the D-metric chosen does not already include rebounding value. DRAPM's factor split includes dREB; EPM's tracking SPM includes rebounding. If using D-EPM/DRAPM, reduce or drop the separate rebound term for defence and keep rebound credit on the offensive side only (offensive rebounds already enter Oliver ORtg via points produced). This is a calibration decision, not a guess to bake in silently.

Playmaking term, from tracking passing data (nba_api)

Replace 0.5 × (AST − TO)/G with a creation term built from potential assists and secondary assists:

PotAST/G  = potential assists per game   (LeagueDashPtStats, Passing)
SecAST/G  = secondary ("hockey") assists per game
Creation/G = AST/G + 0.5 × (PotAST − AST)/G + 0.5 × SecAST/G  [PROPOSAL]
PlayTerm/G = 0.5 × (Creation/G − TO/G)
             + 0.25 × ScreenAST_pts/G ... (optional)

Rationale: (AST − TO) credits only passes that ended in a make, punishing creators whose teammates miss and creators who make the pass before the assist. Potential assists stabilise faster than assists (more events per game) and capture creation gravity; screen assists credit bigs whose value is currently invisible except via REB. Keep the 0.5 coefficient on the (Creation − TO) core so the scale stays comparable to current RPR; the 0.5 weights on missed-potential-assists and secondary assists are PROPOSAL, they must be fitted against RAPM/EPM, and if they do not improve out-of-sample prediction they should be set to zero. A cheaper variant was considered and rejected: adding 0.5 × (AST − TO)/G + 0.15 × (AST/G) × (teammate eFG% on assisted shots − league eFG%) is just shooting luck relabelled. Use PotAST or do not change the term. Also available from the same pull, for a diagnostic (not RPR) column: drives per game, touches, pass rate, and gravity/leverage scores where published, which explain why a playmaking term moved, without entering the formula.

Shot-quality term, from shot location

Option A, shot-diet adjustment to ORtg (recommended):

xeFG% = Σ_zones (league FG% in zone z × player's FGA share in zone z)
        zones: rim (<5 ft), mid-range, corner 3, above-break 3
ShotDietPts/G = 2 × (xeFG% − league eFG%) × FGA/G
  Positive when a player takes better shots than average,
  regardless of makes.
ORtg_adj_shot = ORtg_adj + ShotDietPts/G × 100 / PossUsed/G

This rewards shot selection (a repeatable skill) and does not reward make/miss luck.

Option B, shooting-luck haircut (diagnostic, then optional):

ShootingLuckPts/G = 2 × (eFG% − xeFG%) × FGA/G
RPR_reported = RPR_v2 − 0.5 × ShootingLuckPts/G_shrunk  [PROPOSAL]

At 15 games, eFG% − xeFG% is dominated by variance (3P% needs ~750 attempts to stabilise). Shrinking it 50%+ toward zero is the minimum honest treatment; the cleaner choice is to report shooting luck as a separate column ("RPR, of which ~X is shooting over expected") and not subtract it from the headline number until validation shows the subtraction predicts better. Do not build a "shot-making over expected" leaderboard into RPR itself.

On/off and lineup term: do not add as a term

OnOff_shrunk = OnOff_raw × PossOn / (PossOn + 1000)  [PROPOSAL, diagnostic only]

No additive on/off term in RPR at any weight before a full season, and none at all without garbage-time filtering and teammate adjustment; at that point it is RAPM, so use RAPM instead. Use on/off only as a flag: if |RPR rank − OnOff rank| is extreme, investigate (role change, injury return, lineup quirk) rather than "fixing" RPR toward on/off.

Durability / availability: report separately, do not bake in

RPR is a per-game rate; it already correlates 0.64 with minutes share, so folding minutes in again double-counts availability and coach trust. Instead publish two numbers:

RPR         (per-game rate, as now, "how good when he plays")
RPR_value   = RPR × GP / 82            [PROPOSAL, "how much value delivered so far"]
  or WinsAdded-style: RPR × Poss/G × GP / 100 / (points per win ≈ 30)

RPR_value is the GM-board number (who has actually helped win games); RPR is the rating number. Do not multiply RPR by minutes share inside the rating, that is how a durable mediocrity outranks a brilliant part-timer, and it is a presentation choice, not a measurement. Games missed through injury should also be shown as GP and a "provisional" flag, not silently imputed as replacement level inside RPR (replacement level is the right imputation for the team-rating aggregation in Validation, not for the player rating).

What each black-box metric would do if imported wholesale (not recommended)

RPR_blend = 0.5 × RPR_standardised + 0.5 × EPM_standardised  [NOT RECOMMENDED]

Standardise each to mean 0 / SD 1 across qualified players, then blend. This would almost certainly improve RPR's correlation with future wins: EPM/LEBRON/DARKO/BPM all beat raw RAPM out-of-sample, and blending a box metric with a plus-minus metric cancels partly-uncorrelated noise. But it converts RPR from a transparent, explainable rating ("here is exactly what each point comes from") into a 50% black box whose defence and playmaking components cannot be explained, audited, or debugged, and whose values change when the model owner retrains their models. If the project's goal is prediction, do the blend and rename the product. If the goal is a transparent hobby rating, keep RPR's own formula, use EPM/LEBRON/DARKO only as the defence prior at low weight and as validation targets, and say so explicitly wherever RPR is published.

Try the mechanics

Two small calculators, using exactly the formulas above. Nothing is sent anywhere; they run in your browser.

3P% shrinkage

Shrunk 3P% = (Makes + m × LeagueRate) / (Attempts + m), with m = 750 by default. Trust in the raw figure = Attempts / (Attempts + m).

Shrunk defence term

Possessions per game ≈ MIN/G × pace / 48. DefPts/G = D-impact × Poss/G / 100. At λ = 0.25 the term that enters RPR is a quarter of that (the rest is the box-score prior).

Validation: before and after any change

The ready-made harness: hoop-impact-bench

Use hoop-impact-bench (open benchmark, v1.1.0, Oct 2026) instead of inventing a validation protocol. It takes prior-season ratings and scores them on the next season's games: 7,230 games, 2020–21 to 2025–26, 180 team-seasons, with 2,000 bootstrap resamples.

pip install hoop-impact-bench
hib --ratings my_metric.csv
  # CSV: season,nba_id,rating (per-100, known before opening night)

Tests: (1) game-margin R², (2) team win% correlation r, (3) team net-rating error (RMSE, points, also grades a metric's scale), (4) rebuilt-roster subset (third of games/teams keeping least of last season's minutes, the post-trade test in miniature). Rules that matter: players with <250 minutes the prior season (including rookies) count as −2.0; a team's rating is 5× the minutes-weighted average of its players using minutes actually played; ranks are reported as ranges over resamples, and "gaps under about .006 [in R²] are within the noise".

Validation protocol for RPR specifically

  1. Correlation with established impact metrics (concurrent validity). Compute Pearson/Spearman corr(RPR, EPM), corr(RPR, LEBRON), corr(RPR, DARKO DPM), corr(RPR, BPM), corr(RPR, multi-year RAPM) on qualified players (≥500 possessions), this season and past seasons. Expect RPR to correlate most with BPM/SPM (same inputs) and less with RAPM-family metrics; a defence change should specifically raise corr(RPR, D-EPM / DRAPM) without raising corr(RPR, BPM). If it only raises the BPM correlation, it added no new information. Report correlations by component (offence vs. defence term separately).
  2. Correlation with team wins and Net rating (aggregation test). Aggregate RPR to team level exactly as hoop-impact-bench does (5× minutes-weighted average, actual minutes, replacement −2.0 for unrated/low-minute players) and correlate with team win% and opponent-adjusted Net rating (use Adj Net, not raw Net). Do this in-sample and out-of-sample (first-half RPR vs. second-half Adj Net). A player rating that cannot aggregate to team performance is not measuring impact.
  3. Out-of-sample prediction (the real test). Split-half / forward prediction: fit any weights (λ, shrinkage K, playmaking coefficients) on games 1–~40 of past seasons, test on the remainder; or fit on season t, test on season t+1 (the hoop-impact-bench design). Never fit and test on the same games. Future performance: does RPR at game 15 predict RPR/EPM/RAPM at game 82 better than RPR without the new term? Report R² gain with bootstrap CI. Post-trade / rebuilt-roster test: hoop-impact-bench's rebuilt-roster subset, or manually, players who changed teams, does pre-trade RPR predict post-trade team Adj Net movement (teammate-adjusted) better than the old RPR? This is the closest test to "does the rating travel with the player, or was it his team?" A defence term that only works for players who stay put has not measured defence. Game-margin test: submit RPR (converted to per-100, pre-season version) to hoop-impact-bench and compare R² / win% r / net error against the published rows, with paired bootstrap intervals (hib --pairs).
  4. Stability across samples. Split-half reliability: randomly split each player's possessions/games into halves; correlate RPR halves. Report by component, the defence term will be least stable, the scoring-efficiency term most. A component with split-half r < 0.3 at 15 games should not carry weight >0.25. Rolling stability: recompute RPR weekly; report mean absolute rank change per week and the share of players moving >20 ranks on one week's games. A "better" RPR that whipsaws weekly is worse for a GM board. Year-over-year repeatability: for past seasons, corr(RPR_t, RPR_t+1) for qualified players, vs. the same for EPM/BPM/RAPM. Single-season RAPM's 0.322 Spearman is the cautionary reference.
  5. Ablation / placebo. Remove each new term in turn and re-run tests 1–3. If removing the term does not worsen out-of-sample prediction, the term is decoration, drop it even if it "looks right". Also run a placebo: a term built from shuffled tracking data should not improve anything; if it does, the validation pipeline is leaking (e.g. minutes or team strength smuggled in through the shuffle).

What to report with every change: a one-page changelog entry, what changed, the exact formula, the weight and how it was chosen (which seasons, which fit), and a table of the four tests above (old RPR vs. new RPR, with bootstrap intervals and n). Keep the old RPR computed in parallel for at least a month so regressions are visible. Version the data files by date (EPM/DARKO/SportsDataverse snapshots change under you).

Minimum test a new component must pass to earn its place

All three, on data the component's weights were not fitted on:

  1. Out-of-sample gain: it improves forward prediction (game-margin R² or second-half Adj Net correlation) by more than the bootstrap noise floor, practically, a paired R² gain whose 90% interval excludes 0, and larger than ~0.006 (the hoop-impact-bench noise threshold). A concurrent-correlation gain alone (e.g. "corr with EPM went up") does not pass. Concurrent correlation can be manufactured by copying EPM.
  2. No stability regression: split-half reliability and week-to-week rank churn do not get worse; the component's own split-half r at the current sample size is reported, not hidden.
  3. No duplication: ablation shows the component adds predictive information beyond the box-score terms already in RPR (test: residualise the new term on existing RPR components; the residual must still predict). If it only re-weights AST/STL/BLK, it is a re-skin, not an improvement.

If a component fails any one of the three, it stays out, even if it fixes a "real" blind spot in theory. A theoretically-motivated term that does not survive this test is a hypothesis, not an improvement.

Pitfalls

  • Overfitting the formula to one season (especially this one). With ~15 games per team, it is trivially easy to find weights that "fix" 2026–27's surprises and fail everywhere else. Every coefficient must be fitted on past seasons (2021–22 to 2025–26) and frozen before being applied to 2026–27; the current season is test data, not training data. If a weight was chosen by looking at this season's leaderboard, say so and discount the validation accordingly.
  • Chasing noisy single-season RAPM. Single-season RAPM repeats year-over-year at Spearman 0.322; its standard errors at 15 games are larger than the differences between most rotation players. Treating a +2.0 DRAPM at game 15 as a fact (rather than a shrunk prior with a wide interval) will manufacture false "breakout defenders" and false "washed" stars, and will reverse itself by January. Always publish the shrinkage weight and, ideally, a standard error or "provisional" flag alongside any RAPM-derived term.
  • Laundering a black-box metric into RPR and losing transparency. Importing EPM/LEBRON/DARKO at full weight makes RPR unexplainable: when a reader asks "why is Player X rated +3?", the honest answer becomes "because Dunks & Threes' model says so", and when that model is retrained, RPR changes for reasons nobody on the project can state. It also creates a hidden dependency: EPM v1 retires at the end of this season, LEBRON's methodology can change, DARKO is one person's project. Cap any imported metric at low weight (λ ≤ 0.25 early season), label it in every display ("includes D-EPM defence prior, weight 25%"), and keep a pure-box-score RPR computed in parallel so the rating never depends on a vendor.
  • Small-sample shooting noise. 3P% needs ~750 attempts for reliability 0.7 and does not reach reliability 0.5 even at 480 attempts in split-half data; TS% needs ~169. At 15 games a player has ~60–90 3PA and ~150–250 total scoring attempts. Any formula that takes raw 3P%/TS%/ORtg at face value in November is mostly ranking luck. Shrink, separate shot diet from shot making, and show attempts alongside every percentage.
  • Collinearity / double-counting. The traps: STL+BLK and D-EPM (both contain steals/blocks); AST and potential assists and EPM's SPM (assists enter three times); REB and DRAPM's dREB; minutes and per-game volume and a durability multiplier. Every new term must be checked against the terms it overlaps with (variance inflation / correlation matrix of components) and netted against the term it replaces. A component correlation above ~0.7 with an existing component is a red flag that it is a duplicate.
  • Ignoring availability. A per-game rate that ignores GP will rank a 10-game star above an 82-game star and mislead a GM board whose job is wins delivered. But the fix is a separate value column, not a hidden minutes multiplier inside the rating, otherwise availability is counted twice (once in per-game volume via minutes share, once in the multiplier) and the rating quietly becomes a playing-time leaderboard (the existing 0.64 correlation with minutes share is already a warning sign). Also: do not impute missed games as league-average inside RPR; replacement level belongs in team aggregation, not in the player rating.
  • Fragility as a silent failure mode. The most likely way this project breaks is not a wrong formula but a dead feed: stats.nba.com blocks the host IP, nba_api breaks on a header change, the SportsDataverse 2026–27 file is a month stale, D&T retires v1. Design for it now: cache every external pull with its date, keep ESPN as the always-on fallback, make every new term optional (RPR computes cleanly with the term missing, falling back to the box-score prior), and alert/log when a source is stale rather than silently serving last week's numbers.
  • Garbage time and role changes. On/off, RAPM, and tracking matchup stats are all polluted by garbage time and by players changing roles (injury fill-in vs. regular rotation). Any possession-level work should exclude garbage time (e.g. margin >20 in the 4th, or use CTG-style filtering as a reference) and should be split by role/position where possible. A player's "defence" measured in 100 garbage-time possessions against third-stringers is not his defence.
  • Scale confusion. Per-100-possession impact (EPM, RAPM, D-EPM) and per-game value (RPR) are different units. The most common formula bug in this area is adding a per-100 number directly to a per-game number. Every conversion must go through × Poss/G / 100 and be sanity-checked: a +2.0 per-100 defender playing 60 possessions is worth ~+1.2 points/game, not +2.0.

Where this project is: RPR v1.2 was steps 1 and 2 of the fix; v1.3 (23 Nov 2026) is step 3

RPR v1.3 (23 Nov 2026), kept in parallel since 28 Nov 2026. Three changes on top of v1.2, from a fresh-eyes audit of the Nov 22 run: (1) defense half-weighted, 0.5 not 1.0, because at full weight it was 46% of Wembanyama's RPR and put Dyson Daniels 9th on steals alone; (2) the counting terms are shrunk by total minutes, MIN / (MIN + 150), so a 1-block-in-2-minutes cameo cannot fabricate a per-36 rate; (3) no RPR under 5 games or 50 total minutes, and OppAdj K re-calibrated from 500 to 150 for November possession counts. On the Nov 22 data Wembanyama goes 7.76 to 5.23 with a 26.5% defense share, Daniels falls from 9th to 22nd, and Curry rises to 4th. The v1.3 numbers for the current run are in the README run summary alongside the v1.2 headline, not in this snapshot page.

Step 1 of the sequence above, shrinkage + opponent adjustment, is already built in this repo as RPR v1.1. As implemented in player_profiles.py: ORtg is shrunk toward the league-average ORtg with K = 200 possessions used (split-half calibration on this season found the best K = 150 by MSE, with the curve essentially flat from 150 to 250, so 200 is the midpoint); OppAdj is shrunk toward 0 with K = 500 possessions faced; TS% and 3P% are also shrunk toward league rates (3P% m = 750 attempts). The old values are kept as rpr_v1 and rpr_v1_1 in the CSVs, and RPR_value = RPR v1.2 × GP / 82 is reported separately. No prior-season ORtg is used: the shrinkage pulls every player toward the same league average, rookies and veterans alike.

RPR v1.2 (Nov 20, 2026) recenters the counting terms. v1.1's flaw: the scoring term was capped by the ORtg gap while AST-TO, STL+BLK, and REB were unbounded positives, so only 8 of 469 players were negative and the median was +2.19. v1.2 centers each counting term on its minutes-weighted league-average per-36 rate (playmaking 0.88, defense 1.98, rebounding 1.63 on the Nov 20 run; total 4.49 per 36), so an average player scores about 0 from those terms. The weights (0.5, 1, 0.25) are unchanged: the fix is centering, not re-weighting. The scoring center is the season league ORtg on the player scale (100.25 on the Nov 20 run, 100.18 on the Nov 22 run), not 100 and not the team-scale 111.1; the two scales are never mixed. Position-specific centers were tested and rejected: a rebound is worth the same whoever gets it, and centering by position erased the real difference between a center who rebounds and a guard who does not (it dropped Jokic to 8th). Old values are kept as rpr_v1 and rpr_v1_1 in the CSVs and JSON.
