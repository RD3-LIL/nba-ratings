BUCK: best bang for your buck

Data through November 27, 2026 (271 games). 407 players with a salary and an RPR, 407 matched of 482 in the box scores, 181 qualified for the Top 25 below. Salaries fetched 2026-11-28.

BUCK = RPR ÷ average salary over the remaining contract, in $M. RPR v1.2 is the output, a per-game value above an average player, already scaled by minutes, so 0 is average and +3 to +7 is a star. The input is the average yearly salary over the years left on the deal, including this season, not just this season's salary. BUCK now, shown for comparison, is RPR ÷ this season's salary only. BUCK total is RPR_value ÷ average salary, where RPR_value is RPR times GP / 82, so it rewards games played.

That is how years left enter the formula, with no invented weight. A cheap year before a big extension is not a cheap player, and a flat cheap deal stays cheap. His RPR is near 0 on this run, so his BUCK is near 0 too: BUCK inherits RPR's early-season noise, and a player whose RPR moves by 0.5 moves his BUCK by about 0.18 on a $2.85M deal, so read a single run's BUCK as a snapshot, not a stable value.

Read it as value, not as a pick. A negative BUCK is a below-average player, however cheap he is: cheap does not make a below-average player good value. The top of this list is role players on small deals, not stars, because salary varies about 40x across the league while RPR varies about 10 points. A star can be worth every dollar at a low BUCK and still be the player you want. Early-season RPR is noisy, and the future salaries are taken as listed in the Basketball-Reference contracts table, which does not label options, so the average can move when a contract option is declined or exercised.
