BUCK: best bang for your buck

Data through November 22, 2026 (248 games). 406 players with a salary and an RPR, 406 matched of 475 in the box scores, 182 qualified for the Top 25 below.

BUCK = RPR ÷ average salary over the remaining contract, in $M. RPR v1.2 is the output, a per-game value above an average player, already scaled by minutes, so 0 is average and +3 to +7 is a star. The input is the average yearly salary over the years left on the deal, including this season, not just this season's salary. BUCK now, shown for comparison, is RPR ÷ this season's salary only. BUCK total is RPR_value ÷ average salary, where RPR_value is RPR times GP / 82, so it rewards games played.

That is how years left enter the formula, with no invented weight. A cheap year before a big extension is not a cheap player, and a flat cheap deal stays cheap. Read it as value, not as a pick. A negative BUCK is a below-average player, however cheap he is: cheap does not make a below-average player good value. The top of this list is role players on small deals, not stars, because salary varies about 40x across the league while RPR varies about 10 points. A star can be worth every dollar at a low BUCK and still be the player you want. Early-season RPR is noisy, and the future salaries are taken as listed in the Basketball-Reference contracts table, which does not label options, so the average can move when a contract option is declined or exercised. Some salaries look surprisingly low, and they are as listed, not a parse error: LeBron James is $3,876,529 for Philadelphia in 2026-27, and 32 players are listed at exactly $2,449,421. Both were checked against the cached Basketball-Reference page on Nov 21, 2026, so BUCK uses them as listed. 106 players have total remaining more than $10M above guaranteed.
